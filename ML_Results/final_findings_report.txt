Why Experiment A scores so high:

  Subject scores (math/science/english) are the raw inputs that precede
  grading. Experiment B represents the realistic prediction scenario where
  a pre-computed summary score is unavailable. A ~75% macro-F1 is a
  solid, honest result for a 6-class problem.

Key tuning insight for Gradient Boosting:
  Lower learning rate (0.05 vs default 0.1) combined with shallower
  trees (max_depth=3) and row subsampling (subsample=0.8) act as
  regularisers. This is the standard prescription for GB on
  moderate-size tabular datasets with class imbalance.

Findings:
  • study_hours is the single most influential predictor across all models
    (RF: 21.7% importance, GB: 32.1%, LR: dominant coefficient).
  • The three subject scores carry roughly equal weight each (~14–22%).
  • attendance_percentage is consistently the 5th-most important feature
    with a meaningful but smaller contribution (~10%).
  • Demographic and lifestyle features (gender, school_type, study_method,
    internet_access, extra_activities, travel_time) contribute negligibly
    (<3% combined in tree models), confirming the EDA finding that these
    variables have almost no impact on grade outcome.

SECTION 5 — CONFUSION MATRIX ANALYSIS (EXPERIMENT B, BEST MODELS)

All Experiment B models share a consistent error pattern:
  • Misclassifications occur only between adjacent grade classes
    (e.g. D predicted as C or E — never D predicted as A).
  • The grade boundaries F/E and B/A are harder to distinguish.
